## Video Player

A picture the app gives, the controls over it, and a layer of the app's own over the picture: an ordinary player, or only the picture with the app's own controls, as in a vertical feed. Nothing is decoded here: the app draws its frame as an element and holds every value of the player; the player tells it what a person asks for.

```rust
use lurq::ui::{VideoPlayer, VideoPlayerSize};

// Bound to the app's signals: a press sets the signal, and then the handlers are told.
VideoPlayer::new()
  .media(frame)
  .duration(250.0)
  .buffered(140.0)
  .playing(playing.clone())
  .position(position.clone())
  .volume(volume.clone())
  .muted(muted.clone())
  .preview(move |at| thumbnails.frame_at(at))
  .on_position_change_end(move |to| decoder.seek(to))
  .mount(ctx);

// Held by the app: a press changes nothing until the app answers by setting the prop.
VideoPlayer::new()
  .media(frame)
  .duration(250.0)
  .playing_controlled(is_playing, move |play| decoder.set_playing(play))
  .position_controlled(at, move |to| decoder.seek(to));

// The picture alone, with the app's own controls over it.
VideoPlayer::new().media(frame).controls(false).child(my_controls).mount(ctx);
```

- A press on a control tells the app what the value is to be and changes nothing by itself: bound, the signal is set first; held, the app answers with the next render. No handler is called when the app sets a value, nor for a choice of what is already chosen.
- **Forms.** The large form has the seek bar over a row of play, the volume (its slider folds into its button under 480), the time, then captions, the speed ("1×"), settings, picture in picture and fullscreen. The compact form, under 400 wide, has play, the time, mute, a "more" menu of captions, speed, quality and picture in picture, and fullscreen. The minimal form, under 280, is one row: play, the seek bar and the elapsed time. Mounted, the form follows the width it is laid out at from the first frame (`ElementRef::observe_rect_before_paint`); `size` says another. `VideoPlayerSize::for_width` gives the form of a width, and `VIDEO_PLAYER_COMPACT_BELOW`, `VIDEO_PLAYER_MINIMAL_BELOW` and `VIDEO_PLAYER_VOLUME_FOLDS_BELOW` are the widths.
- **Layers**, bottom to top: the picture (and the poster), the veil, the app's layer, the middle (the 64 overlay play button while paused, 48 in the compact form; a spinner while it waits; replay when it ended; the error), the captions' line, the poster's length, and the bar on its gradient. A press of the picture plays and pauses at once; a second press within `VIDEO_PLAYER_DOUBLE_PRESS` (500 ms) asks for fullscreen too. The app's layer and the middle let a press through where they hold nothing.
- **Hiding.** While it plays the bar fades out `VIDEO_PLAYER_HIDE_AFTER` (3 s) after the pointer rests, over the slow duration, and the pointer is hidden over the player (`CursorIcon::None`); both come back on a move of the pointer, on pause, while the keyboard's focus is in the player and while a menu is open, over the base duration. Paused, waiting, ended and failed keep them. The captions' line moves down to 12 over the bottom while the bar is hidden.
- **Keys**, while the focus is in the player: Space and K play and pause, Left and Right seek 5 s (`VIDEO_PLAYER_SEEK_STEP`), J and L 10 s, Home and End go to the start and the end, Up and Down move the volume by 0.05, M mutes, C turns the captions, F asks for fullscreen. Space and the arrows are the controls' own while the keyboard's focus is on one of them. A press of the picture gives the player the focus.
- **Menus.** Settings holds Quality (when `qualities` are given), Speed (not live) and Captions, each with its value at its end; choosing a row shows its choices in the menu's place under a row that goes back (the menu's pages), and choosing one closes the menu. The speed button opens `ui::MediaSpeedMenu`; the compact form's "more" holds captions, speed and quality (each with its page) and picture in picture. They hang over the seek bar, their end on the bar's end, and stay the theme's.
- **Seeking with a preview.** Mounted, the seek bar shows the time under the pointer and, where `preview` is given, the frame for it over the time (`Media/Seek Preview`).
- Everything over the picture is white on black at the board's alphas, the same in a light and a dark theme: the picture is not themed, so themed controls would lose their contrast on it. The literals are in the recipe. The menus, the tooltips and the live badge are the theme's.
- Every control has a tooltip with its key (mounted) and a name to what reads the tree; the toggles say `a11y_pressed`, the menu buttons `a11y_haspopup` and `a11y_expanded`. The player is a `group` called by its label.
- Parts: `VideoPlayerPart::Root`, `Media` (the picture's layer; its cursor is the pointer's while it is pressed to play), `Veil`, `Overlay`, `Center`, `Spinner`, `Error`, `ErrorIcon`, `Text`, `Title`, `Description`, `Retry`, `Captions`, `Caption`, `Chip`, `ChipValue`, `Controls` (its background is the color its gradient ends in), `Row`, `Control`, `ControlIcon`, `Speed`, `Time`, `Elapsed`, `Menus` (its gap is how far a menu stands over the row), `SettingsMenu`, `MoreMenu`, `MenuValue`. States: `VideoPlayerState::Playing`, `Waiting`, `Ended`, `Error`, `Live`, `Muted`, `Captions`, `Fullscreen`, `Poster`, `Bare`, `Hidden`, `Narrow`, and `On`, resolved for a button of the bar that is on, under the pointer too. Sizes: `VideoPlayerSize::Sm`, `Md`, `Lg`.
- In the design (`Video Player` `yKk3P`, `Compact` `cak7v`, `Minimal` `OUkUD`) the large player is 640 by 360 with the large radius. Its bar is 88 high, padded 24 at the top, 12 at the sides and 8 at the bottom, 4 between the seek bar and the row, on a gradient from nothing to `#000000B3`; the row is 36 high, 4 between buttons of 36 with white glyphs of 20, the middle radius, `#FFFFFF1F` under the pointer, `#FFFFFF33` while on, and a white ring of 2 from the keyboard. The time is mono 12 in the large bar and 11 in the compact one. The compact bar is 72 high, padded 20 8 6, a row of 28 with 2 between buttons of 28 and glyphs of 16; the minimal one is a row 48 high padded 16 8 4 4, 6 apart. The settings are 240 wide, the compact menu 200. The captions' line is 12 over the bar and 24 from the sides, 16/500 white with a line of 1.4 in a box of `#000000B3` padded 2 8. The poster's length is mono 11 in a box of `#000000B3` padded 2 6, 12 from the bottom and the end. The veil is `#00000099`. The error is a white glyph of 24, a title 14/500, a line 13 in `#FFFFFF99`, and a white button 32 high.
- A renderer that draws another player starts from the view: `view.root()` (the box with its behavior), `media()`, `veil()`, `overlay()`, `center()`, `captions()`, `chip()`, `bar()`, or `view.bar` for the controls made (`VideoPlayerBar`: the seek bar, play, the volume, the time, the buttons at the end and the menus).

## For `CHANGELOG.md`

- `ui::VideoPlayer`: a video player of the design system that draws the app's picture with the board's controls over it in three forms that follow its width (large, compact, minimal), or the picture alone with the app's own layer over it; every value it shows is the app's, bound or held, and told back on a press, a drag or a key. `ui::Menu` has pages (`MenuItem::page`, `Menu::page`), and `CursorIcon::None` hides the pointer.

## Decided

- **Fullscreen fills the app's window, not the monitor** (Ivan, 2026-11-16: "fullscreen of a video is the video over the whole of the app, not the app over the whole monitor"). `VideoFullscreen::Window` is the default: the player is in a layer over its whole window and the window stays as it is on the desktop. `::Screen` takes the window to the whole screen as well, for an app that wants it. It replaces what the paragraph on real fullscreen below says of the default. The tooltip of the volume is its button's alone: one around the whole control showed "Mute" over the slider (Ivan, the same day).
- **Fullscreen and picture in picture are of a window, and two players of one value are in both** (Ivan, 2026-11-16: fullscreen in the playground's own window took the main window there too, and closing the second left the first in full screen). The gallery's playground bound the player on its stage and the one in its own window to the same signals: right for the position and the volume, wrong for these two. The own window's player has signals of its own for them. An app with one video in two windows does the same. The player also takes the window it is rendered in when it goes to full screen, asked for at that render, and not the one its component was created under: the root of a second window is mounted before that window is its own.
- **The touch form is a form of this component, chosen by the app** (`touch(true)`), from the board's `Mobile/Video Player` (`Md4tS`) and `/Landscape` (`HFTze`). `lurq::ui` has no rule yet for when a component is its mobile one (a density, a pointer that is a finger), so nothing chooses it by itself: that rule is the design system's to make, and this prop is what it would set. Upright: no radius; a scrim of black at 40% while the controls show; a top row of 44 with the captions and the settings (round, 44, glyphs of 24) and, where the app listens, a button that makes the player small; in the middle ten seconds back and on (44, glyphs of 28) 32 from a play of 56 with a glyph of 32 on black at 40%; a bottom row of 44 with the time and fullscreen; the seek bar 16 from the sides and 6 from the bottom, its thumb 16 (`SeekBarSize::Lg`). On its side, while in fullscreen: 44 at each side for the notch, a way back, the title (16/600) and its line (13 at 60%), the transport 56 apart at 48 and 72, the speed in a pill of 44 in the bottom row, the seek bar 56 from the sides. No volume: a phone has keys for it. A tap on the picture shows the controls and hides them, paused or not; the pointer moving does not show them. Two taps within half a second on the left or the right third seek ten seconds and show the mark, half an ellipse of white at 20% from that side with the glyph and "10 seconds"; each tap after adds ten. **Differs from the board**: the controls come and go at once, with no fade; the settings and the speeds are the desktop's menus, hung under the top row and over the bottom one, where a phone would have a sheet; the home indicator is not drawn (the system draws it). Not checked on a phone: the gallery shows it under a mouse.
- **Picture in picture is real.** A mounted player whose `picture_in_picture` is true opens a window 400 wide and as high as its aspect makes it (`VIDEO_PLAYER_PICTURE_WIDTH`), with no frame, kept over the other windows of the desktop, named `picture-in-picture`. In it is the player itself, as large as the window, in its minimal form (play, the seek bar, the time), told the same values and telling the same handlers; a strip of 36 at its top moves the window, and a button at its end asks to leave. Where the player stood it keeps its room and says "Playing in picture in picture" over black, with a button that brings it back; the error's glyph, title and button parts draw them. The window closes when the value is false again, when the player is unmounted, and asks to leave when the system closes it. A player in fullscreen is not in picture in picture. `picture_in_picture_window(false)` leaves the window to the app. **The picture is given to the other window as the element it is**: a picture that is a mounted component of the first window is not one there. Real playback is to give a frame both windows can draw. The window has one size and is not resized by its edges. Not checked on macOS or Linux.
- **Fullscreen is real.** `fullscreen` was a value the player told and drew a glyph for. A mounted player whose `fullscreen` is true is now in a layer over its whole window (the engine's `Modal` on the root), as large as the window, its corners square, in the form its new width gives it; and it takes the window to the whole screen (`WindowHandle::set_full_screen`) and gives it back when it leaves or is taken away. Where it stood a box keeps the room it had, so nothing around it moves and it comes back to the same place. Escape asks to leave, as F and the button do; the app still answers by setting the value. `fullscreen_fills(VideoFullscreen::Window)` fills the window alone, and `::App` leaves it all to the app, which is how the gallery draws the state in its place. A window that draws its own chrome (`components::WindowChrome`, as the gallery's does) kept its title bar over the player in fullscreen, and its close button there closed the window in place of leaving the full screen (Ivan, 2026-11-16): the engine's chrome now draws no title bar while its window is in full screen, as a window of the system's own has none. A player with no controls has no key of its own, Escape among them.
- **A track of captions is read here, and the player finds its cue.** `ui::MediaCaptions::parse(text)` reads the text of a WebVTT or a SubRip file into cues (`MediaCue { start, end, text }`, in seconds): the header, `NOTE`, `STYLE` and `REGION` blocks, a cue's number or name and WebVTT's settings of where a cue stands are passed over, tags (`<i>`, `<v Ada>`, `{\an8}`) are taken out, and what cannot be read is left out with the rest kept. `MediaCaptions::at(time)` is what is said then, cues that hold together on lines of their own. `VideoPlayer::caption_cues(&track)` shows it; `caption_text` stays for an app that finds the cue itself. Not read: where a cue stands (`line`, `position`, `align`), its styles, and more tracks than one: the app gives the track of the language chosen, and `captions_track` its name. Nothing loads a file: the app gives the text (`include_str!`, or what it read).
- **A folded volume shows its slider under the pointer, in the time's place** (Ivan, 2026-11-16: "I see only mute"). Under 480 the large bar has no room for the volume's slider and it folds into its button, as the board draws. The player built the control still, so the slider never came out and the volume could only be moved by the keys. The player now keeps what the control needs between renders (the pointer over it, the keyboard's focus in it, its slider dragged): while the slider is out the time is not drawn, since the bar has room for one of them, and it folds again once the pointer leaves. The compact form (under 400) has the mute button alone, as the board draws it: the keys Up and Down move the volume there.
- **A player keeps a shape, and its width is the most it takes where the app says.** `aspect(f32)` is the picture's width over its height (16 by 9 unless told, `9.0 / 16.0` for a feed's upright one): a player with no height of its own is as high as that makes it at the width it has. `max_width(Length)` lets what holds it be narrower than its width, a window as narrow as a phone: `width(432.0).max_width(Length::Fill)` is 432 by 243 in a wide room and 326 by 183 in a narrow one, in its compact form there, from the first frame. The gallery's states and its feed are sized so; its Sizes keep each width, which is what they show, and scroll sideways. The playground's stage scrolls to what is wider than it and so has no width to be a share of: the page tells the player the stage's room (`Playground::stage_room`).
- **Every value is named, none is "the" value.** A player holds nine values of the app and no one of them is what the player is, so none takes the plain `on_change`: an `on_change(bool)` on a player would not say what changed. Each value follows the rule for a second value of a component: `x(Signal<T>)` bound, `x_controlled(value, handler)` held, `on_x_change(handler)` to add a handler (`playing`, `position`, `volume`, `muted`, `rate`, `captions`, `quality`, `fullscreen`, `picture_in_picture`). `new()` takes nothing, since a player is drawn before it has anything to play. The bound forms take the signals the shared parts take (`Signal<f32>` for the position, the volume and the rate, as `SeekBar` and `MediaVolume`), and the handlers are told `f64`, as theirs are.
- **Seeking tells twice**: `on_position_change` as the handle moves, `on_position_change_end` where it is let go, the names of the rule for what is dragged ("While it moves and when it ends"); `on_volume_change_end` likewise. A key that seeks tells both, so an app that seeks only on the end hears every key.
- **The picture is `media(..)` and the app's layer is `child(..)`**, by the rules: a picture a component is given is `media`, free content is `child`/`children`. The poster is `poster(..)`, an element too, and so is the frame of `preview`.
- **States the app sets are plain values** (`waiting`, `ended`, `live`, `error(title)` with `error_description` and `on_retry`): the player shows them and has nothing to tell of them. Replay tells `playing` true.
- **A mounted component.** It keeps a timer (the bar fading 3 s after the pointer rests), its measured width (the form and the folding of the volume, before the paint), the open state of its menus and the page shown, whether the time reads what is left, when the picture was last pressed, its tooltips and the mounted seek bar, which need a `Ctx`. None of it is the app's.
- **A component by itself again, and now a kept one** (Ivan, 2026-11-16, after the line under this one). `VideoPlayer` implements `app::component::IntoComponent`, and the engine converts whatever does into an element that declares its component (`Element::component`): `.child(VideoPlayer::..)` compiles again, and is the host `mount(ctx)` mounts, mounted by the component that renders the tree once its render has returned. So the form without a `Ctx` keeps what the mounted one keeps: the form follows its width from the first frame and its menus open (tested). `mount(ctx)` stays, for mounting at once; `build(theme)` stays the still picture. What the line under this one says of `.child(..)` not compiling is no longer so; its reason is what this form answers.
- **Mounted, the only way** (Ivan, 2026-11-16). `VideoPlayer` is not an element by itself: there is no `From<VideoPlayer> for Element`, and `.child(VideoPlayer::..)` does not compile; an app writes `.mount(ctx)`. The form that became an element without a `Ctx` kept nothing between renders, and was the one in which a slider lost its drag after the first change it made. `build(theme)` stays as a still picture for a test or a screenshot, said by name.
- **A press of the picture plays at once, and a second one soon after asks for fullscreen.** The engine's double press holds the first press back for its interval (500 ms) to tell a double press from it, and a player that answered a press half a second late would read as broken; the player counts its own presses instead, as a browser's player does, so a double press also plays and pauses again.
- **The form follows the width, and the height follows a width given alone**, at 16 by 9, from the first frame: a player given `width(Length::Fill)` has the height of its picture. Given both, it is as told.
- **The bar's gradient is drawn from the part's background**: `PartStyle` has no gradient, so `Controls`' background is the color the gradient ends in at the bottom, and the renderer draws from nothing to it.
- **The menus hang from the bar's row**, `Placement::TopEnd`, `Menus`' gap (24, 22 compact) above it: their bottom 4 over the seek bar and their end on the bar's end, as the board has the settings menu. The row is no trigger, so a press of a menu's button while it is open closes it (the press outside), and the next press opens it. The settings and the compact menu are 240 and 200 wide over the menu's own least width of 230.
- **Pages in `ui::Menu`, not in the player**: the settings' rows show their choices in the menu's place, which the board draws; a menu kept by the player beside `ui::Menu` would have been a second menu. An item's page and its back row share a key so that the focus survives the swap; no request of the focus from a handler was needed.
- **On stays on under the pointer**: a button that is on keeps `#FFFFFF33` under the pointer (`Selector::new().state(On).hovered()`), or the settings' button would read as off while the pointer is on it with its menu open.
- **Captions and the error are part of the controls**: without controls only the picture and the app's layer are drawn, as the brief has it; an app of a feed draws its own.
- Thresholds: compact under 400, minimal under 280 (the board's 432 state examples are the large form, its compact one 320, its minimal 200); the volume folds under 480, the board's number.
- The keys act while the focus is within the player; the player is focusable without being a stop of Tab, so a press of the picture gives it the focus. Space and the arrows are left to a control that has the keyboard's focus (a button presses on Space, the seek bar moves on the arrows); the letters always act.
- A faded bar is checked in the tests by what the window composites: a group with an opacity under 1 is painted into a layer at that opacity and its quads keep their own, so the tests read the layer the bar's rect is in (`render_pass_with_app`), not the quad.
