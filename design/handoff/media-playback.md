## For `docs/src/content/docs/design-system.md`

## Playing a video

`VideoPlayer::source(&Source)` (feature `media`) makes a mounted player play what the source holds: a file, bytes, a resource of the app, an address.

```rust
VideoPlayer::new()
  .source(&Source::file("clips/lake.mp4"))
  .width(640.0)
  .max_width(Length::Fill)
  .mount(ctx)
```

The player opens the source with the app's loader, decodes it with the system's decoder and draws its frames as its picture. Where it is, how long it is, and whether it plays, waits, ended or failed are the media's, in place of what the app would say. A press on a control tells the app's handlers and does what it says to the media: play, pause, the seek bar, the speed, the keys. An app that holds `playing` or `rate` (a signal, or a value it sets) moves the media by changing them. With no source the player is drawn from the values it is given, as before.

An app that draws its own player asks for a media itself:

```rust
let media = Media::open(app.loader(), &Source::file("clips/lake.mp4"));
media.play();
let picture = media.frame().map(Video::new); // drawn again by itself as frames come
let state = media.state(); // duration, position, size, playing, waiting, ended, error, rate
media.seek(42.0);
media.set_rate(1.5);
media.on_change(|| { /* it opened, ended, failed: from the media's thread */ });
```

## Playing a sound

`AudioPlayer::source(&Source)` (feature `media`) makes a player play what the source holds: a file, bytes, a resource of the app, an address.

```rust
AudioPlayer::new(position, 0.0)
  .source(&Source::url("https://example.com/night-drive.mp3"))
  .title("Night Drive")
  .volume(volume)
```

The player opens the source as a sound and plays it through the system's output. Where it is, how long it is, and whether it plays, loads or failed are the sound's, in place of what the app would say: the length given to `new` is drawn only until it opened. A press on a control tells the app's handlers and does what it says to the sound: play, pause, the seek bar, the volume, the mute, the speed. An app that holds `playing`, `volume`, `muted` or `rate` moves the sound by changing them. With `AudioRepeat::One` the track starts over at its end. "Try again" loads the source anew.

A sound at an address is loaded whole through the app's loader (its headers, its timeout, what it allows; the feature `http`), and kept: played again, by this player or another, it starts at once and asks the network for nothing. While it loads the player is drawn loading. What is kept is what the server sent, up to 64 MiB of sounds; over that, what was played longest ago is let go.

```rust
sound_cache().budget(256 * 1024 * 1024); // keep more
sound_cache().forget(&source);           // this one has changed: load it again
sound_cache().clear();
```

That is kept in memory, for the run of the app. An app that gives a folder has what it loads from addresses kept there as files, for its next run too:

```rust
// The folder is the app's own cache folder: lurq picks none.
let app = App::new().enable_media_disk_cache(cache_folder);
app.loader().media_disk_cache().budget(2 * 1024 * 1024 * 1024); // 512 MiB unless told
```

A sound then plays from its file and is not kept in memory as well, and a player of a sound an earlier run kept opens it as it is drawn. `sound_cache()` goes on answering whether a sound is kept and how much is, and `forget` and `clear` delete the files too; its `budget` is of the memory alone. Over the folder's budget the file used longest ago is deleted. What is kept is not asked of the server again, however old it is: an app that knows a sound changed forgets it.

An app that draws its own player asks for the sound itself:

```rust
let song = Media::sound(app.loader(), &Source::file("songs/night-drive.mp3"));
song.set_volume(0.8);
song.play();
song.set_muted(true);
let state = song.state(); // duration, position, sound, volume, muted, playing, waiting, ended, error, rate
```

A video has its sound too: `Media::open` and `VideoPlayer::source` play it, and the picture goes by what is heard.

## Decided

- **A frame stays on the graphics card** (Ivan's rule from the start: a frame never goes through the CPU's memory, and one copy from GPU to GPU is fine; built 2026-11-16, after the sound he asked for first). It was two stages: the first copied a frame through memory once, to have the decoder, the clock and the player's binding working; this is the second. On Windows, under the `wgpu` renderer on Direct3D 12:
  - **The renderer says which card it draws on** (`wgpu_render/card_frames.rs`, `announce`): where its device has NV12 textures (`wgpu::Features::TEXTURE_FORMAT_NV12`, which the engine now asks for where the card has it), it gives the card's identifier to `images::set_card_frames_adapter`. Nothing else does, so the `dx12` renderer, a test and a window with no device yet are "nothing draws on a card".
  - **The decoder decodes on that card** (`media/mf.rs`, `OnCard`): a Direct3D 11 device on the same card, safe for the decoder's threads, given to the source reader (`MF_SOURCE_READER_D3D_MANAGER`). Its frames are then textures of the card (here 640 by 368 for a picture of 640 by 360, in an array of 24).
  - **One copy, on the card**: the picture's part of the decoder's frame is copied (`CopySubresourceRegion`) into one of three NV12 textures shared by handle (`images::CardFrames`, of surfaces `CardSurface::Nv12`), and the frame is shown once the card has done the copy (an event query), so what draws never reads a frame half copied. The three take turns: the one shown, the one being written, and one between them.
  - **The renderer opens each texture once** on its own device (`OpenSharedHandle`, then `wgpu_hal::dx12::Device::texture_from_raw` and `create_texture_from_hal`), and draws the two planes of the one that is shown with the NV12 pipeline it had: `CachedImageTexture::Card`. Direct3D 12 opens such a texture for simultaneous access, so neither side has to hand it over. Nothing is sent to the card for a frame, and nothing of it is read.
  - **`Media::frame()` gives another kind of `ImageData`**, with `native()` of the backend `CardFrames`; nothing above it changed: `components::Video` and the player draw it as they drew the frames of memory.
  - **A media that opened before anything drew on a card starts over on the card.** A player on an app's first screen opens its video before the window has a device, which the first run of the gallery showed: one media was on the card and one on memory for good. For ten seconds after it opens, a media whose frames go through memory looks ten times a second for something that draws on a card, and then is opened again with that card's device where it was, showing what it showed until its frame there is decoded. Its sound's output is opened anew with it: a moment's gap if it plays, which at an app's start it does not.
  - **Through memory still**, as before: where nothing draws on a card, where the decoder gives memory all the same (a software decoder), where the card has no Direct3D 11 device, and on every renderer but `wgpu` on Direct3D 12. A window on another card than the one that announced itself does not open the textures, and draws nothing of the video: one card is what was built.
  - **No new dependency**: `wgpu`'s own `hal` is what opens the texture, and the `windows` crate both use is one. The feature `media` has `windows/Win32_Graphics_Direct3D11` more.
- **A decoder of the app's own gives its frames the same way** (Ivan, 2026-11-16: Parties is to be written in Rust, decodes by itself with NVDEC, AMF and others, and "feeds a texture into our video"; its tiles are plain pictures in a grid, with no controls). `images::CardFrames` is what lurq's media gives and what such an app gives, and one path draws both. What its C++ renderer takes from its decoders was read first (`DecodedFrame` in its `encdec/include/encdec/decoder.h`, and its `docs/video-pipeline.md`), and each part of that has its place here:
  - **Three kinds of surface** (`CardSurface`): one NV12 texture (lurq's media), a texture of gray and one of color apart (its NVDEC path: `R8` and `R8G8`), one texture of red, green and blue converted on the card (its AMF path). Each by the handle it is shared by; the renderer reads the size and the format from the texture itself, and opens nothing that is not what its kind says.
  - **The picture may be a part of its texture** (`CardFrame::crop`): a decoder's texture is as large as what is coded (1920 by 1088 for 1080). With no crop said, the picture is the part at the top left that is as large as the image. An edge of the picture inside the texture is drawn from half a pixel within it, a whole one where the frame is gray and color (the color is at half the size), so that nothing past it is mixed in.
  - **A frame is drawn once it is written, and nothing waits for it** (`CardFrames::with_fence`, `CardFrame::ready_at`). In each of its frames the renderer takes the newest frame shown whose value the fence has reached, and draws the one before it until then. The other way was looked at and works (a probe, not kept: wgpu gives the Direct3D 12 queue behind it, and `ID3D12CommandQueue::Wait` on it for a fence of another device holds the work wgpu submits after it until the fence is signalled, for a texture of red, green and blue, of gray, and of color). It was not built: a decoder that never signals would hold the whole window's frame, and the price of not waiting is one frame of delay at most.
  - **A frame's texture is given back when the card has finished with it** (`CardFrame::lease`): something of the decoder's, dropped once another frame has taken its place and all the work that drew it is done (`Queue::on_submitted_work_done`, after the renderer's next submit), or at once where another took its place before anything drew it. Frames no longer on the screen are let go of the same way. A decoder writes only in a texture it was given back.
  - **An image of the card's frames is another picture with each frame shown**: its version is the count of frames shown, so a decoder calls `CardFrames::show` and nothing more.
  - **The card to decode on** is `images::card_frames_adapter()`, and `None` where nothing draws frames of the card: the app's decoder gives memory then (`StreamingImage`), as lurq's media does.
  - **A decoder on another card than the one that draws is shown all the same, through memory** (2026-11-17; a laptop with two cards, where NVDEC is on one and the window on the other). A texture shared by handle is opened by a device of its own card only, so the renderer's device opens nothing. Then a Direct3D 11 device is made on each card of the machine, once (`wgpu_render/card_copies.rs`, `Readers`), and the one that opens the first surface opens them all and their fence (`Copied`). A frame whose fence is reached is copied on its card into a texture memory reads (`CopyResource` to a staging texture, the gray and the color of an NV12 texture from the one), asked for in each of the renderer's frames without waiting (`Map` with `D3D11_MAP_FLAG_DO_NOT_WAIT`), and sent to textures of the renderer's own (`Queue::write_texture`), which are drawn as the card's are: the same two pipelines, the same crop. One frame is copied at a time, and the one before it is drawn until it has come. A frame's lease is let go of as soon as it is read, for nothing draws the decoder's texture. Direct3D 11 and not a second device of wgpu: a staging texture reads an NV12 texture as it is and says without waiting whether the copy is done.
  - **The frames say how they come** (`CardFrames::way`): `Card`, `Memory`, or `Nowhere` where no card opens them or a surface is not what it is said to be; `None` until they are opened. Frames that nothing opens are kept as such and not asked for again in each frame. An app that is told `Memory` may decode on the card that draws, and one told `Nowhere` gives its frames another way.
- **A frame decoded before a pause is not shown after it.** Whether a media plays is read at the moment a frame is due, not as the thread's turn began: `Media::pause` is in the state at once, and the test that read the picture's version just after it failed now and then ("and no frame comes").
- **The media is the truth of a player that has a source.** Its time, its length and whether it plays are read from the media at each render; the app's values for them are not drawn. What a person presses is done to the media by the player itself, after the app's handlers heard of it, so a player given a source and nothing else is whole. What the app says of `playing` and of `rate` moves the media when it changes from one render to the next. An app that holds `playing` and refuses a press (`playing_controlled` whose handler does nothing) still has the media play: the press is the person's.
- **A media has a thread of its own, and a clock of its own.** The thread decodes one frame ahead and shows it when the clock comes to its time; a frame more than 0.2 s late is passed over. The clock is where it was and when, going at its speed while it plays: `state().position` is read from it, with no frame needed. Pause and seek change the state at once, so a seek bar being dragged does not see the time go back; the thread then goes to the last place it was sent to, not to each.
- **A new frame draws the window and renders nothing.** The image is one that asks for continuous redraw (`ImageData::streaming_nv12`), which the engine already has for video. The player itself is rendered four times a second while it plays, for its time and its seek bar, and when the media says it changed.
- **Seeking is to the frame.** The reader goes to the key frame before the time, and the frames from there to the time are decoded and not shown.
- **Fullscreen and picture in picture draw the same media.** The player in the small window is given the picture and no source, so nothing is opened twice.
- **Sound** (asked for by Ivan, 2026-11-16: "sound first, with a cache, loaded from bytes, from a local file and from a link"). On Windows the reader's first audio stream is decoded to plain samples (PCM, or float where the stream is float), brought to two channels, and given to the default output in shared mode. The media's thread does both the picture and the sound: it keeps a quarter of a second of sound decoded ahead and the device a fifth of a second.
- **The clock is the sound's.** A media that has sound and somewhere to play it sets its clock to what the device says was heard, where the two are more than 30 ms apart; nearer than that the clock goes on by itself, which is smoother than the device's steps. So the picture follows the sound, a pause stops both at once, and a media with no sound, or with no device, goes by the clock it had.
- **The sound starts with the picture it was sent to.** After a seek the output is given nothing until the frame at that time is shown.
- **The volume and the mute are of the media's own stream** (`IAudioStreamVolume`), not of the samples: they are heard at once, and not a fifth of a second later. They are linear, as a browser's are. Muted, the volume is kept.
- **A speed plays the sound that much faster, and higher.** The output is opened anew with the samples said to be that many times as many in a second, and the system brings them to the device's rate. What the old output held and had not played is given to the new one, so nothing is skipped. The pitch is not kept: that needs the samples stretched in time, which is not built.
- **A device that goes away** (unplugged, or another one made the default) is answered by opening the output again on the one the system has now; with none, the media goes on unheard.
- **What is only heard is opened as a sound** (`Media::sound`): its picture is not decoded, and an address is loaded whole and kept. A video at an address is still read by the system as it plays, so that it starts before it is all there; a sound is small enough to load first, and loading it is what makes it possible to keep.
- **The cache keeps what the server sent, in memory, for the process** (`sound_cache()`). This is the cache of an app that has not turned the one on the disk on, and it stays beside that one (see "The cache on the disk" below). It is kept, by the source's `cache_key`, so `Source::key` names a sound whose address changes. The loader keeps no bytes by its own rule ("what is decoded from them is kept by who decodes"); a sound is decoded as it plays, so what it keeps is the encoded bytes. 64 MiB unless told, the one played longest ago let go first, one larger than the whole budget not kept. Nothing is written to the disk by it: with no folder given, a sound is loaded again by the next run of the app. A file and bytes are not kept: they are where they are.
- **Where the loader cannot give the bytes, the system reads the address itself**: larger than `Loader::max_bytes`, or a build without `http`. Nothing is kept then. Every other failure of the load is the media's error, in the loader's words.
- **Bytes that came by a name are opened with it** (`MF_BYTESTREAM_ORIGIN_NAME`), so that the system's reader for `.mp3` is asked for what ends in `.mp3`; bytes with no name are told from themselves.
- **A sound at an address is loaded when it is first played, not when its player is drawn.** A list of a hundred players of a hundred addresses loads nothing until one is played; until then a player is drawn from the length the app gave it. A press of play opens it, and it plays once it is loaded. A file, bytes and a sound that is kept already are opened as soon as the player is in a tree, so their length is shown.
- **The clock is not set to the sound while a seek is on its way.** `Media::seek` moves the state at once and tells the thread; between the two, what is heard is still where it was, and setting the clock by it would send a seek bar back for a moment. The media counts the times it was sent, the thread the ones it has done.
- **`AudioPlayer::source` is `VideoPlayer::source`'s way**: the media is the truth of where it is, how long, and whether it plays; the app's values for those are not drawn. `loading` is the media's `waiting`, the error line is the media's reason. `AudioRepeat::One` starts the track over, because that is of one sound; `All` and shuffle are of a queue, and the app's.

### The cache on the disk (built 2026-11-16)

Ivan asked for it ("we need it as a feature, and if it is off, keep in memory"; "launch a parallel thread that does this, and then I will sort it out"), gave how it is turned on, and named the method while it was built: `enable_media_disk_cache`. What follows was chosen by the session that built it, each with its reason, for him to change.

- **It is turned on with `App::enable_media_disk_cache(self, folder) -> Self`**, which takes the app and gives it back, as `App::with_tokio_handle` does. Not called, nothing is written to the disk and a sound is kept in memory, as before: every test there was passes unchanged. It is a setting of the app, not a feature of the build, and lurq picks no folder. The loader's own names follow his: `Loader::enable_media_disk_cache`, `Loader::media_disk_cache`, `MediaDiskCache`.
- **It lives in the loader, and keeps everything `Loader::load` fetches from an address**, as the images session's note of the loading layer has it: a picture at an address (`ui::Picture` loads through `app.loader().load`) is kept as a sound is, and read from its file by the next run. **The name says "media"; whether a picture is to be kept is Ivan's to say.** It is one arm of `Loader::start` (the one for an address): taken out, the cache is of sounds alone, since a sound's own path writes its file too (`media::settle`).
- **What does not go through it**: the old `ResourceLoader` (`Image::from_resource`, background images, the part images of a checkbox and a slider, resource SVGs), and a video at an address, which the system reads as it plays.
- **The budget is 512 MiB unless told, and is told to the cache**: `app.loader().media_disk_cache().budget(bytes)`, which reads as `sound_cache().budget(bytes)` does. Every loader has the cache, off until it has a folder, so the budget may be told before or after it is turned on, and there is no `Option` to open. 512 MiB is eight times the memory's 64: about a hundred songs, or eight of the largest things the loader reads whole (`Loader::max_bytes`, 64 MiB), and not much of a phone.
- **Turning it on deletes nothing for being over the budget.** What an earlier run left is cut to the budget when something more is kept, or when the budget is told. Otherwise an app that keeps 2 GiB would lose all over 512 MiB between its two calls.
- **A file is named by its source and its length**: `{cache_key:016x}-{len:x}`, with the ending of its address where that is a sound's or a video's (`9f3a41c2d07be815-8a3f10.mp3`). The length in the name is what tells a file somebody cut short, with no index file beside the files to keep in step with them. The ending is a hint for a system's player and for a person looking into the folder; no other ending is written, so nothing in the folder has a name the system would run (an address may end in `.exe`). Media Foundation tells a sound from its bytes whatever its name is: tried with no ending and with a wrong one.
- **The key is `Source::cache_key`, which std's `DefaultHasher` makes of the address.** It is the same in every run of one build. std does not promise it from one compiler to the next: a build with another compiler may name the same address otherwise, and then the old files are not found, are counted, and are deleted as the ones used longest ago. A key of the app's own (`Source::key`) is as stable as the app makes it. A hash of lurq's own in `Source` would settle it; `resources/source.rs` is the images session's, and was not touched.
- **A file is written under another name and renamed when it is whole** (`key-process-count.part`, flushed to the disk first), so one with a kept file's name is never half-written. A `.part` an hour old was left by a run that ended, and is deleted when the cache is turned on.
- **Only its own files are its own.** What has no such name is not counted, read or deleted: the folder may hold other things of the app. `clear` deletes the cache's files alone.
- **A file that is gone or cut short is loaded again, not played.** Its size is asked of the system when it is opened (one call) and when the cache is turned on, and held against the length in its name. One that is as long as it was and is not what was written (the disk spoiled it) is found by the decoder: the media says it cannot play it, its thread deletes the file, and "Try again" loads it anew. The content is not hashed: that would read the whole file at every open.
- **`holds` answers from what the cache knows and reads nothing**, because a player asks it at every render. A file somebody deleted is found missing when it is opened.
- **Two loads of one address at once** are one load in one loader, as before. Two loaders on one folder (two apps, two processes) each write their own `.part` and rename it: the last one wins, and both are whole. What another process wrote after this one turned its cache on is not known to it until its next run. The folder is meant to be one app's.
- **The one used longest ago is deleted over the budget**, and a use is a sound opened from its file or a load read from it. It is kept from one run to the next as the time the system says the file was changed, which is set at each use; on Windows with the right to write a file's times alone, which is given while the file plays (the right to write the file is not). What is larger than the whole budget is not kept in the folder: a sound is then kept in memory, as with no folder.
- **A file that plays can be deleted**, and plays on to its end: Windows lets a file Media Foundation reads be deleted and replaced (tried, 2026-11-16), and the other systems always did. So forgetting and the budget do not wait for a player.
- **Forgetting deletes the file.** `sound_cache().forget(&source)` lets go of it in memory and on the disk. `Loader::forget(&source)` deletes the file too: it is the one way a picture has to be loaded anew, and it is what a player's "Try again" calls. `sound_cache().clear()` and `MediaDiskCache::clear()` delete every file of the cache.
- **`sound_cache()` answers for both caches**, as was promised: `holds`, `forget`, `clear`, `bytes` and `len`. So `AudioPlayer::source` (which opens a sound at an address before it is played only where it is kept) and the gallery's line needed no change, and a player of a sound an earlier run kept opens it as it is drawn. **`budget` is the one that does not**: it is of the memory alone, since the folder's budget is another number, and of everything the loader keeps, not of sounds. It knows of a folder by following the app's cache when `App::enable_media_disk_cache` is called, and a loader's when a sound is first opened with it; two apps in one process are answered for together. `bytes` and `len` count the folder whole, whatever is in it.
- **A sound plays from its file** (`Opened::File`) where the cache is on, and its bytes are let go once it is loaded. While it loads they are in memory: the loader reads a load whole and then writes it; it does not write as it reads.
- **Nothing fails for the cache.** A folder that cannot be made or written is one warning in the log, and what is loaded is kept in memory as with the cache off.
- **What is kept is not asked of the server again, and is not let go for being old.** The server's own word (`Cache-Control`, `ETag`) is not read. In memory a new run of the app was a fresh load; on the disk there is none until the file is deleted for room or forgotten. **An address whose content changes is to be forgotten by the app, or given a key of its own.** This is the choice Ivan is most likely to want otherwise once pictures are kept.
- **What came with a header is kept as it came.** The key is the address alone, as the cache in memory has it: a sound fetched with `Authorization` is a plain file in the folder. An app that must not leave such things on the disk does not turn the cache on; a source cannot say "do not keep me" yet.
- **A browser keeps what it loads itself**: nothing is written there, and the call does nothing.

### The loader on the app's tokio runtime (built 2026-11-16)

Ivan asked whether the loader's thread pool works on tokio; it did not, and he asked for it.

- **The app tells its loader.** `App::with_tokio_handle` and `App::set_tokio_handle` give the handle to the app's loader too (`Loader::tokio_handle`), and `App::clear_tokio_handle` takes it away (`Loader::reset_tokio_handle`). A loader made by itself has none until it is told, and reads on its own threads. In a build without the feature `tokio` nothing changed.
- **A load is a blocking task of the runtime** (`spawn_blocking`), with `ureq` and the readers as they were. It is the small change: one code path for what is read, no new dependency, and the same progress, timeout, limit, stopping and wakers, since the same closure runs. An async client in place of `ureq` would be a second client to keep.
- **Four loads at a time there too**, as the loader's own threads read (`LOADER_THREADS`), by a semaphore. With no limit a page of two hundred pictures would be two hundred threads and two hundred connections at once: the runtime's blocking pool goes up to 512. A load waits for its room as a task of the runtime, so the runtime is to be one that runs its tasks, as the components' async tasks need already.
- **The loader's own pool is not made** where the runtime is used: it was always made by the first load that needs it. A load that was on its way when the handle was given ends on the thread that reads it, and the ones asked for after it are the runtime's. A handle taken away gives the next loads to the pool.
- **A load given to a runtime that stopped fails with `LoadError::Cancelled`**, and does not wait for ever: the read that was dropped unrun ends its load (`Unread`). It stays failed until it is forgotten, as any failed load does. An app that stops its runtime while it runs calls `clear_tokio_handle` first.
- **The media's own thread (`lurq-media`) is not on tokio**, and was not asked to be. It is not a job: it lives as long as its media, and blocks on the decoder and on the output device in a loop of its own. As a blocking task it would hold one of the runtime's blocking threads for as long as a song is on the screen.

### A media tells who listens when it starts to play (fixed 2026-11-16)

A player asks its media whether it plays when it is rendered, and runs its clock (four beats a second) only while it does. The media's thread set that it plays and woke nobody: a player rendered after a press of play and before the thread heard the command was told no, and nothing rendered it again. Its time stood at `0:00` while the sound went on. On a busy machine `given_a_source_it_plays_the_sound_and_its_controls_move_it` failed most runs with "it never came to: playing" (six of six on `claude/ds-media` as it was, with none of this work); a media played beside it in the same test went on to its end. `mf.rs` and `apple.rs` now wake who listens when the thread starts to play.
