# Changelog

## Unreleased

Breaking:

- A border on some sides of a rounded element no longer thins out round a corner whose other side has none, as CSS draws it: it goes on round the arc at its full width and fades out along it, from opaque at the bordered side to nothing at the other. Sides with the same placement and dash are now one shape whatever their colors, and two colors that meet at a rounded corner go over from one to the other along the arc, at full coverage; at a square corner they meet on the diagonal, where the side painted last covered the corner. A border outside or centered on the element can paint past a side with none by its width near such a corner. Layout and hit testing are unchanged, but every app with such a border is painted differently. `StrokeStyle` has a new field, `side_colors`, and `StrokeStyle::outset`. See [styling](docs/src/content/docs/styling-events.md#borders).
- A value that changes because the theme does, a color of the other appearance, is applied at once to an element that has a transition for it, as it is to everything else in the window. Such an element used to fade from its old color while the window around it had already changed: a button stayed light for its transition's length on a window that had turned dark. A transition is for what the element does itself: a state, a style set on it.
- The light palette's greys are darker than the design's first ones, which left a light window all but white: `Canvas` `#e6e6eb`, `Muted`, `Secondary` and `Highlight` `#d9d9e0`, `SecondaryHover` `#c8c8d1`, `Border` `#bdbdc7`, `MutedForeground` `#52525b`. `Canvas` is what is behind panels and cards: an app that paints its window with `Background` and its cards with `Card` has white on white in the light appearance.
- **The default theme is the design system's.** `Theme::new()` has the design's palette (`pencil/lurq-design-system.pen`) in place of the blue one of 0.61: a neutral primary, other borders, a near-black foreground. It has a dark palette too and follows the system's appearance, so on a system in its dark look a window is dark: text that names no color is near-white, and a window whose root paints no background is cleared to the dark `Background`. The radii are the design's too: `Sm` 4, `Md` 6, `Lg` 8, where they were 3, 5 and 6. So is the type scale: body text is 14 where it was 16, so `Text::new(...)` with no style is smaller, headings are semibold with tighter letter spacing, and `TypographyStyle::Mono` is 13. An app that paints light backgrounds of its own under text that names no color must say what it wants: `app.theme().apply(ThemeDefinition::legacy())` brings the whole 0.61 look back, with one palette, and `app.theme().set_preferred_appearance(Some(Appearance::Light))` keeps the new look in its light palette. See the [migration guide](docs/src/content/docs/migration-v0-62.md).
- `ThemeTypography` has two fields more, `sans` and `mono_family`: one written out field by field must add them.
- `ThemePalette` has a field for each of the design's roles (42 in place of 23). A palette written out field by field with no `..ThemePalette::default()` must add them; build one from `ThemePalette::light()`, `dark()` or `legacy()` instead.
- `Theme::set_palette_color(role, color)` sets the role in the light palette and in the dark one, so a color set once holds when the system changes its appearance; `Theme::set_palette(palette)` sets the palette of the appearance the theme is in and keeps it when the appearance changes and comes back. On a theme with a palette for each appearance both were lost at the next change of appearance.
- A window whose root paints no background is cleared to the theme's `Background`, which is white in the light palette as before and near-black in the dark one. It was always white.
- A text built with `Text::styled(..)` from a style that keeps the default text color names no color, and takes the `.foreground(..)` of an element above it, as a text with no style does. It took the theme's foreground whatever was above it.

- A disabled element hears the pointer come and go: its `on_mouse_enter` and `on_mouse_leave` run as the pointer comes onto it and leaves it, so a tooltip can say why it is disabled. They did not run. Nothing else of the pointer reaches it: it is not hovered, and no press, click or drop reaches it.
- A modal over the whole window (`Modal::target(ModalTarget::Root)`) in a window with a `WindowChrome` covers the window under the title bar, where it covered all of it with its top under the bar: a dialog is centred in what is seen, and a veil ends at the bar. What is passed to `WindowChrome::overlay` still has all of the window.
- An element that is disabled while it has the focus, inside a focus group, passes the focus to the element before it in the group (or the one after it) where the focus was lost.
- An element that leaves the tree while it has the focus, inside a focus group that stays, passes the focus to the group's stop that took its place (the next or the last) where the focus was lost.
- `BackgroundColor` has a variant more, `CurrentForeground`: a `match` on it must take it.
- `AnimatableValue` has two variants more, `Percent` and `Dash`, and `AnimatableProperty` one, `BorderDash`: a `match` on them must take them.
- `AnimatableValue` has a variant more, `Fit { px, content }`: a `match` on it must take it.
- `FocusAxis` has a variant more, `None`: a `match` on it must take it.
- A single-select whose checkmark is after the label keeps the checkmark's room on the selected row only: the labels and trailing elements of the other rows run to the row's end. Every row kept an empty slot of the checkmark's width.
- `KeyboardEvent` has a field more, `focus_within`: one written out field by field must add it.
- `FormValue` has a variant more, `Range(f64, f64)`: a `match` on it must take it.

Deprecated:

- The palette roles of 0.61, each in favor of one of the design's: `Accent` → `Primary`, `AccentHover` → `PrimaryHover`, `AccentMuted` → `Highlight`, `SurfaceBase` and `SurfaceInput` → `Background`, `SurfacePanel` → `Canvas`, `SurfaceRaised` → `Card`, `BorderStrong` → `Input`, `BorderFocus` → `Ring`, `TextPrimary` → `Foreground`, `TextSecondary` and `TextMuted` → `MutedForeground`, `TextInverse` → `PrimaryForeground`, `Danger` → `Destructive`, `DangerMuted` → `DestructiveMuted`. The `PaletteColor` variants and the `ThemePalette` fields still compile, with a warning that names the replacement. Each keeps a color of its own, and setting a role also sets the deprecated roles it replaces, so `Select`, the form controls and a checkbox or slider with no style of its own, which still read them, follow a theme set through the new roles. `PaletteColor::replacement()` gives the role that replaces one.

Added:

- `lurq::ui`: the design system's components and what a theme styles them with. A component is a builder that goes where an element goes and takes the theme of what renders it (`ui::current_theme()`), so it needs no context passed to it and is built again when the theme changes.
- `ui::Recipe`: how the parts of a component look, case by case. A recipe is a list of rules, each a case and the style of a part in it: always (`base`), for a `variant`, for a `size`, in a window `at` a breakpoint or wider, in a `state` of the component, `hovered`, `pressed` or `focus_visible`, or several of these together (`when(Selector::new().variant(..).hovered(), ..)`). The rules that hold are laid the more specific over the less. Variants, sizes, states and parts are names, so an app adds its own.
- `ui::PartStyle`: the style of one part, every field optional: background, foreground, border, radius, shadow, a `veil` over the fill, outline or the theme's `focus_ring`, opacity, padding, gap, width and height (pixels or a step of the control, density or icon scale), the text (a typography role and any field over it, in one of the theme's two families or a named one) and transitions with the theme's durations and easings. Rules for the pointer and the keyboard become state styles of the node, so a hover renders nothing again.
- A theme says what it wants of a component: `Theme::patch_recipe::<C>(..)` lays rules over its recipe, `Theme::set_recipe::<C>(..)` gives it one in place of the design's, `Theme::set_renderer::<C>(..)` builds other nodes for the same behavior, and `Theme::set_part_renderer::<C, _>(part, ..)` builds one part. `ThemeDefinition::ui` (`ui::UiDefinition`) holds the same for a definition. `ThemeDefinition::bare()` and `Theme::bare()` have the design's tokens and no recipe: every component comes out unstyled and still works, as the base for a look that shares nothing with the design's.
- The rest of the design's buttons, each on the recipe and renderer of the theme: `ui::Toggle` (a button that stays on: `on(bool)`, `on_change`), `ui::ButtonGroup` (buttons joined in one line around them, or filled and a pixel apart: a split button), `ui::ToggleGroup` (toggles of which one, or any number, are on), `ui::SegmentedControl` (one of a few choices on a track), `ui::Link` (text that is pressed, underlined under the pointer) and `ui::Fab` (a floating action button). `ToggleGroup` and `SegmentedControl` bind a `Signal<T>`, or a `Signal<Vec<T>>` for any number, or are `controlled` by the app; a choice is `(value, "Label")` or a `ui::Choice` with an icon.
- `Button::description(text)`: a second line under the label, for the design's compound button. A button that is an icon alone is named by its label for tooling that reads the tree.
- `PartStyle::underline(border)`: a line along the bottom of a part. `ResolvedRecipe::rules_for(part, inner)`: the rules a component that holds another gives it for its place.
- `ui::Icon`: an icon by name, `Icon::new(icons::SEARCH)`. It is a square of a step of the theme's icon sizes (16 by default, or a number of pixels), is drawn in the foreground of what it is in unless it names a color, and turns with `.spin(true)`. `ui::icons` has a constant for each of the 152 icons of lurq's font. The font is loaded, hinted, in every app: `App::load_hinted_font` is for an app's own.
- The theme's icons: `ThemeIcons`, what each name of an icon is drawn with (`IconGlyph`, a character of a font). `Theme::set_icon(name, glyph)` gives a name another glyph or adds a name, `Theme::icon(name)` reads it, and `ThemeDefinition` has the table (`icons`). lurq's components name the icons they draw, so a theme changes them too.
- `ui::SPIN`: the keyframes of a full turn, registered in phase in every window, which a spinning icon plays once in `ui::SPIN_PERIOD`. Keyframe identifiers from `0xFF00` up are lurq's own.
- `ui::Button` is the design's whole button: sizes `Xs` (28) and `Xl` (44) beside `Sm`, `Md` and `Lg`, each with its padding, label and icon; `icon(name)` and `trailing_icon(name)`, an icon of the theme's set in the color of the label; `icon_only(true)`, a square; `shape(ButtonShape::Pill)`, a pill, and a circle for an icon alone; `loading(true)`, a spinner in the place of the icon and no press until it is over. Its recipe has the parts `ButtonPart::Icon` and `ButtonPart::Spinner` and the states `ButtonState::Loading`, `IconOnly` and `Pill`.
- The theme is at a density: `Theme::set_density(Density::Compact)` and `Theme::density()`, `Default` unless set, and part of a `ThemeDefinition` (`density`). A recipe has rules for one, `Recipe::density(density, part, style)` and `Selector::density(..)`, which hold while the theme is at it, between the rules for a size and those for a breakpoint; a component is built again when the density changes. What draws rows of its own reads `theme.density_value(theme.density())`. The design's recipe for `Button` has no rule for a density.
- `WindowControls::hover_foreground(kind, color)`: the foreground one control has under the pointer and while pressed, for a close control that turns white on its red. A control's glyph is drawn in the foreground of the control, as content of `WindowControlContent::element` that names no color is.
- `SliderPartStyle::outline(Outline)`: a ring around a part of the engine's slider, its track, its fill or its thumb, so that `thumb_focused` can be a focus ring.
- Focus groups: `.focus_group(FocusGroup::horizontal())` on an element makes the arrow keys, Home and End move the focus between the stops of Tab inside it. `FocusGroup::vertical()` and `both()` choose the arrows, `wrap(bool)` whether the ends meet, `activate(bool)` presses what the focus moves to (a radio group's choice follows its focus), and `one_stop(bool)` makes Tab stop in the group once, at the element marked `.focus_entry(true)` or the first. A text input, a slider and a select keep their arrow keys.
- `ui::RadioGroup` and `ui::SegmentedControl` are one stop of Tab whose arrow keys move the choice; `ui::ToggleGroup` is one stop whose arrow keys move between its toggles; the arrow keys move between the buttons of a `ui::ButtonGroup`. `RadioGroup::horizontal(bool)` lays the radios side by side.
- `ui::CheckboxGroup`: checkboxes over one list, bound to a `Signal<Vec<T>>` or held by the app, one under the other or `horizontal`.
- The controls of the design system (a button, a toggle, a segment, a link, the track of a switch) are stops of Tab wherever they are, in the order of the tree; a plain `components::Button` stays one only in a form or with a `tab_index`.
- `ui::Switch`: a setting that is on or off. A track with a thumb that slides to the end it is at, a label beside it that is pressed as the track is, `label_hidden` for the track alone, the parts `Root`, `Track`, `Thumb`, `Label` and the states `On`, `Disabled` for a recipe, and `SwitchView::track()`, `thumb()` and `travel()` for a renderer.
- `ui::Select`: one value chosen from a list that opens under a trigger shaped like an input. The engine's select dressed by a recipe, bound to a `Signal<T>` with `(value, label)` pairs or `SelectOption`s, with the sizes and the judgments of an input (`invalid`, `warning`, `valid`), `placeholder`, `full_width` and `disabled`, the parts `Trigger`, `Value`, `Placeholder`, `Chevron`, `Menu`, `Item`, `Check`, `Detail`, `Hint`, `Group`, `Separator` and the states `Open`, `Invalid`, `Warning`, `Valid`, `Disabled`, `FullWidth`, `Chosen`, `ItemDisabled` for a recipe, and `SelectView::select_style()` and `select(style)` for a renderer.
- A field finishes its control: an `Input`, a `Textarea` or a `Select` in it is called by its label and judged as it is, and a press of the label focuses the control. Every control of the design system a form holds takes from a field what it has a prop for (`FieldControl`, `InField`), and a field's description or error is its control's description to what reads the tree. `Field::disabled`; `Textarea` has `read_only`, the sizes `Sm`, `Md`, `Lg` (`TextareaSize`) and `warning` and `valid`; `Input::label`, `CheckboxGroup::label`, `RadioGroup::label`.
- `ui::Slider::orientation` and `length`, and `ui::RangeSlider`: the board's vertical slider, and two thumbs on one track bound to a `Signal<(i32, i32)>` or a `Signal<(f32, f32)>`, drawn by the slider's recipe in the state `SliderState::Range`. `SliderView::values()` and `is_range()` for a renderer.
- `ui::Popover`: a panel beside the button that opens it, over the window, with a title, a description and anything in it. `trigger(button)` makes a button its anchor and its toggle; it takes the focus as it opens and gives it back as it closes, is in the order of Tab right after its trigger, fades in and out, and closes on a press outside it and on Escape. `placement`, `width`, `offset`, `collision`, the parts `Root`, `Header`, `Title`, `Description`, `Body` for a recipe, and `PopoverView::panel()` and `header()` for a renderer.
- `ui::Menu` and `ui::MenuItem`: a list of commands in a panel over the window, opened from an anchor, with icons, shortcuts, separators, group labels, checked, radio, destructive and disabled items and submenus. It takes the focus as it opens and gives it back as it closes; the arrow keys and typing a letter move between its items, Right and Left go into and out of a submenu, and choosing one, a press outside and Escape close it (`Menu::new(anchor, open)`, `MenuItem::on_select`, `MenuItem::submenu`).
- `ui::Dialog`: a panel over a veil that covers the window, the engine's `Modal` dressed by a recipe, with a title, a description, a body, a footer of buttons, a close button, the sizes `Sm`, `Md`, `Lg`, `Full`, and a form whose body scrolls between a header and a footer that stay. It takes the focus as it opens and gives it back as it closes; Escape and a press on the veil close it.
- `ui::Tooltip`: a short text beside an element while the pointer rests on it or the keyboard's focus is on it, mounted on that element with `mount(ctx, element)`. It shows after `TOOLTIP_DELAY`, at once within `TOOLTIP_SKIP_DELAY` of another and for the keyboard's focus on the element or anything in it; the pointer can be moved onto it; it goes after the pointer leaves, on a press and on Escape, which it takes before what is under it; a disabled element shows it too; it is kept in the window, its arrow pointing at the element. `shortcut`, `side` (`TooltipSide`), `arrow`, `delay`, `open` and `disabled`, the parts `Root`, `Content`, `Label`, `Shortcut`, `Arrow` and the states `Arrow`, `Shortcut`, `Multiline` for a recipe, and `TooltipView::content()`, `arrow()` and `aim` for a renderer.
- `ui::Toast` and `ui::Toasts`: a short message in a corner of the window that leaves by itself. The panel with an icon by its variant (`Neutral`, `Success`, `Info`, `Warning`, `Error`), a title and a description, an action (a `Button` or a `Link`), a close control and a timer; and a handle that shows toasts from anywhere, with a host the app's root renders once, that stacks them, holds their time under the pointer, animates them in and out and takes them away after their duration, on a swipe, or on a press. `ui::Button` and `ui::Link` are `Clone`.
- `ui::Tabs`: a list of tabs of which one is selected, with the panel of the selected one, as a muted rail, a row over a line with counts, or pills, along the top or down the side; one stop of Tab whose arrow keys select, an icon, a count, a disabled tab, a divider, and an overflowing form whose list scrolls between two buttons with a menu of every tab; `PartStyle::start_line`.
- `ui::Accordion` and `ui::Collapsible`: headers that open what is under them, one at a time or any number, bound to a signal or held by the app, with Tab and the arrow keys; what opens grows to the height of its content and back, as a transition of `Height` between a number and none now does.
- `ui::Breadcrumb`: the way to where one is, its steps links that a press or Enter follows and the last the page; a chevron or a slash between them, an icon on a step, and with more steps than `max_items` an ellipsis that opens a menu of the hidden ones.
- `ui::Pagination`: the pages of a list with one current, counted from 1, bound to a `Signal<usize>` (`new`, `for_items`) or held by the app (`controlled`). The first and the last page, the current one with `siblings` on each side and an ellipsis where pages are left out, in the same number of slots wherever the current page is (`Pagination::pages_shown`); previous and next disabled at the ends; each a stop of Tab with Left and Right moving between them, a `navigation` with pages called "Page 3" and `a11y_current` on the current one. A `compact` form with "1–20 of 284" or "Page 3 of 12", a `page_size` slot for a `ui::Select` of how many to a page, `summary` and `label` for other words, the parts `Root`, `Nav`, `Item`, `Current`, `Ellipsis`, `EllipsisIcon`, `Summary`, `PageSizeLabel`, `PageSize` and the states `Compact`, `PageSize`, `Disabled` for a recipe, and `PaginationView` for a renderer.
- `ui::Steps`: the stages of a process in order with one current, the steps before it done with a check and a line in the primary color up to it; a description, an icon or an error on a step, in a row or in a column, and steps that are done pressed to go back to them, with Tab and the arrow keys.
- `ui::Progress` and `ui::RingProgress`: how far a piece of work is, as a bar along a rail in four colors and three sizes with a label and its value, a bar that does not know how far it is with a segment crossing its rail (still while motion is reduced), the thin bar across the top of a window, and a ring with the value in its middle whose arc moves round; a width or a height in percent, and a border's dashes, now transition and animate.
- `ui::Badge`: a short label of a state or a count in seven variants and three sizes, round or square, with an icon or a dot before its text, a dot alone, a count with a maximum ("99+") that is nothing at 0, and a badge on the top end corner of another element (the design's Indicator).
- `ui::Tag` and `ui::Chip`: a tag is a label on a thing in five colors and three sizes, with an icon, round as a pill, and a remove button called "Remove" and its text that Tab reaches and Backspace or Delete press; a chip is a tag that is pressed, on or off, bound to a `Signal<bool>` or told of a press, and `ui::ChipGroup` makes several one choice of one or of any number, with one stop of Tab and the arrow keys; `lurq_inspect` reports a node's `a11y_pressed` as `state.pressed`.
- `ui::Avatar` and `ui::AvatarGroup`: who a person is, as initials on a fill, a picture clipped to the avatar's shape (with the initials while it decodes and when it fails) or an icon, in five sizes, round or square, with a dot of presence; and avatars laid over one another, ringed, with the count of those a group leaves out.
- `ui::Card` and `ui::Separator`: a surface with a title, a description and an action in its header, its content and a footer of buttons, outlined, elevated or filled, in two sizes, and pressed as a whole with Tab and the focus ring where the app gives it `on_click`; and a line across a column or down a row, with a word in its middle.
- `ui::Spinner` and `ui::LoadingOverlay`: a ring, dots or bars that say something is being done, at three sizes, in the color of the text around it, with a label beside it, a status to tooling; and a veil with a spinner over a part of the page while it loads, which keeps it from the pointer and from Tab. `Button::loading` turns a `Spinner`.
- `ui::Skeleton`: a line, a circle, a block and a paragraph in the shape of what is loading, as wide as the room they have or as the app sizes them, pulsing or with a shimmer and still where motion is reduced, with `Skeleton::status` to say once what a group of them stands for.
- `WindowHandle::title_bar_inset()` and `set_title_bar_inset(..)`: how much of the window's top the app's own title bar is drawn over. A `WindowChrome` sets it to the height of its title bar, and an app that draws a title bar another way sets it itself.
- `FocusGroup::none()`: a group of no keys, which takes what is in it out of the focus group around it. That group's arrow keys pass it by and do nothing while the focus is in it: the content of an accordion's open item, between two headers.
- `BackgroundColor::CurrentForeground`: a fill that is the foreground the element inherits, the color a text there with no color of its own is drawn in.
- `.offset_by_size(x, y)`: moves an element by shares of its own width and height once it is laid out, on top of `.offset`. `(-0.5, -0.5)` puts its middle where its top left corner was, whatever its size.
- `.stops_inside(true)` on a button makes Tab stop at what takes the focus inside it too, after the button: a card pressed as a whole with buttons of its own.
- `PartStyle::corners(top_left, top_right, bottom_right, bottom_left)`: a radius for each corner of a part.
- A width or a height in percent is animated as a share of the parent's (`AnimatableValue::Percent`), where a transition of it ran from or to nothing; and the dash and the gap of a border are animatable as one value (`AnimatableProperty::BorderDash`, `Transition::border_dash()`).
- `lurq_inspect` reports `state.pressed` for a node described with `a11y_pressed`.
- A transition of `Height` between a height in pixels and none runs to and from the height of the content (`AnimatableValue::Fit`), where the height changed at once: a panel opens and closes with its content's height.
- `PartStyle::start_line(border)`: a line along the start side of a part, beside `underline`, and `PartStyle::min_height(length)`.
- `lurq_inspect` reports `state.selected` for a node described with `a11y_selected`.
- `PartStyle::max_width`; `padding_top`, `padding_bottom`, `padding_start` and `padding_end`, each laid over `padding_y` or `padding_x`, and `paddings()`, the four as they come out; `enter` and `exit`, keyframes a part plays as it appears and as it leaves (`PartAnimation`, with a duration and an easing of the theme's).
- `ui::FADE_IN` and `ui::FADE_OUT`: an opacity from nothing to whole and back, keyframes every window has (`0xFF01`, `0xFF02`).
- `Popup::takes_focus(bool)` and `Overlay::takes_focus(bool)`: the focus goes into the popup as it opens, to the first element in it that takes it, and back to what had it as it closes with the focus inside; while it is open its elements are in the order of Tab right after its anchor's.
- `Modal::takes_focus(bool)`: the focus goes into the modal as it opens, to its first stop of Tab, and back to what had it as it closes, as a browser's `<dialog>` does.
- `.focuses(&element_ref)` on any element: a click of it focuses the first focusable element under the one the ref is on, as a `<label for>` focuses its control.
- `ElementRef::focus_visible_within()` and its signal: a focus that shows its ring on the element or on something in it, CSS `:focus-within` for the keyboard.
- `FocusGroup::follow_pointer(bool)`: the pointer coming onto an element of a group the focus is in moves the focus there, as in a menu. `FocusGroup::typeahead(bool)`: typing a letter moves the focus to the element whose label starts with it, the label being `.focus_label(..)` or a button's one text. `KeyboardEvent::focus_within`: whether the focus is on the node whose key handler runs, or inside it.
- The engine's `Slider` has `orientation(SliderOrientation)` (`vertical()`), laid out, painted and dragged along its height with the least value at the bottom, and `Slider::new_range` / `new_range_f32`: two thumbs on one track, the fill between them, a press moving the nearer thumb, the thumbs not crossing, each a stop of Tab. Every slider takes Home and End (to its ends) and Page Up and Page Down (a tenth of the range in whole steps). `SliderHandle::values()` reads a range's two values, and `set_from_ratio` moves its nearer thumb.
- A range slider in a form: `FormRangeSliderInput`, a field of its own kind (`FormHandle::range`, `range_field`, `range_control`, `ctx.range_control`, `FormValue::Range`, `FormValues::get_range`) submitted as two values under its name, with `FormOptions::validate_range` and `validators::range_span`.
- The engine's select has groups with a label over each and a line between them (`SelectOption::group`, `SelectStyle::group_label` and `separator`), a note at an option's end (`SelectOption::hint`, `SelectStyle::option_hint`), `SelectStyle::trigger_gap`, and a ring on its trigger (`SelectPartStyle::outline`).
- The MCP server: `lurq_set_value` on a range slider takes `[low, high]` and sets both values; `lurq_inspect` reports a node's `a11y_description` as `description`, and `a11y_checked` as `state.checked` for a control drawn of plain nodes (the design system's checkbox, radio and switch).
- `ui::Input` and `ui::Field`: an input is the engine's text input bound to a `Signal<String>` in the design's box, with a placeholder, a leading icon, the sizes `Sm`, `Md`, `Lg`, an edge that says its state (hover, focus, `invalid`, `warning`, `valid`), `read_only`, `disabled` and `full_width`; a field holds any control with its label, a `required` or `optional` mark, a description or an `error` under it, or its label beside it (`horizontal`). Parts and states for a recipe, and `InputView::control()`, `icon()` for a renderer.
- `ui::Textarea`: text of more than one line in the design's box, bound to a `Signal<String>`, with a placeholder, `rows(min, max)` between which it grows with its text before it scrolls, `invalid`, `disabled` and `on_change`; the parts `Root`, `Text`, `Placeholder` for a recipe and `TextareaView::input()` for a renderer.
- `ui::Checkbox`: the design system's checkbox, checked, indeterminate, invalid and disabled, with its label beside it; its state is the app's (`checked`, `indeterminate`, `on_change`).
- `ui::Radio` and `ui::RadioGroup`: one of a few choices that exclude each other. `RadioGroup::new(signal, choices)` is bound to a `Signal<T>` like `ToggleGroup::new`, `RadioGroup::controlled` takes the choice from the app (`None` for none yet); radios are one under the other, 12 apart, with `invalid` and `disabled` for the whole group and `Choice::disabled` for one. A radio is a round mark with a dot while it is chosen and a label that is pressed as the mark is; a press of the one chosen does nothing. - A theme given two palettes with `Theme::set_palettes` changes with the system's appearance, and its typography and bold text follow the person's settings on a phone. `Theme::set_follows_system(false)` leaves the theme as the app set it.
- `LayoutKind` has a new variant, `Grid`: code that matches it without a wildcard arm must add it.
- A border on one side of a rounded element follows the corners at its ends and thins out where it meets a side without a border, as CSS draws it. It was a square band that stuck out past the rounded corners.
- A border outside or centered on a rounded element has the element's corners on its inner edge and is rounder on its outer edge by its width. Its outer edge had the element's radius and its inner edge a smaller one, which left a gap between the border and the element's background at each corner.
- Where the sides of a border have different widths, each inner corner is shaped by the two sides that meet there. Every inner corner was cut by the widest side of the four.
- A side of a border that has no width paints nothing at its edge. At a fraction of a pixel, as on a display scaled by 1.25 or 1.5, the three other sides of a border could leave a faint line there.
- `Border` and `ResolvedBorder` have a new public field, `dash`, and `RectCmd` a new public field, `stroke_style`: code that builds one with every field named must add it. `Border::new`, `inside`, `outside` and `center` are unchanged. A custom `RenderEngine` that ignores `RectCmd::stroke_style` draws dashed borders solid and the corners of an outside border as before.
- `PaletteColor` has a new variant, `Foreground`, and `ThemePalette` a new public field, `foreground`: code that matches the enum without a wildcard arm, or builds a `ThemePalette` with every field named, must add them (`..ThemePalette::default()` takes care of the field).
- An open layer is the layer the same declaration opened in the last pass, wherever it is among the others. Layers without a key were matched by their place, so when the first of two open popups closed, the second took over the first one's scroll offset, focus and node ids. Layers are still matched by place when the declaring element is new. A layer that plays a keyframe animation or has an exit animation is never matched to another declaration's layer: a popup opened as another closes is mounted anew.
- On a machine whose user asked the system for less motion, transitions of place and size no longer animate, sheets and stack navigation included. `Theme::set_reduced_motion(Some(false))` restores them.
- A transition of a color given as a `PaletteColor` role now runs. The value a node is heading to was read from concrete colors only, so a background or border set to a role changed at once whatever its transition said. A role whose color changes with the theme runs the transition as well.
- `AnimatableProperty` has three new variants and `AnimatableValue` two: code that matches either without a wildcard arm must add them.
- `TextStyle` and `MarkdownTextStyle` have a new public field, `strikethrough`: code that builds one with every field named must add it (`..TextStyle::default()` takes care of it).
- Markdown draws `~~struck~~` text with a line through it: `ThemeMarkdown::strikethrough` now sets a strikethrough by default. Set it to `MarkdownInlineStyle::default()` for the old look.
- A plain `Text` whose style has an `underline` now draws it.
- `LayerCmd` has a new public field, `blur: Option<LayerBlur>`, `RenderList` a new public field, `backdrops: Vec<BackdropCmd>`, `AnimatableProperty` two new variants and `QuadContent` a new variant, `Backdrop`: code that builds a `LayerCmd` or a `RenderList` with every field named, or matches `AnimatableProperty` or `QuadContent` without a wildcard arm, must add them. A custom `RenderEngine` that ignores `RenderList::backdrops` draws the frame without its backdrop blurs. A custom `RenderEngine` that paints `RenderList::layers` should paint a blurred layer for `LayerCmd::painted()` (larger than `bounds`, and possibly outside the window) and blur it by `LayerBlur::sigma`; one that ignores the field draws the layer unblurred, clipped to `bounds`.
- A layer around a blurred one covers the blur's spread, so `LayerCmd::bounds` of a faded group can be larger than its own rects and glyphs.
- A glyph whose ink reaches into the clip from a line of text that lies outside it is now painted whenever its text is, for plain texts under 1,024 bytes. Before, it was painted only if the text had first been painted unclipped, and dropped if the text was first painted while the clip cut it.
- Plain texts under 1,024 bytes stay shaped in a cache of their own, up to 4 MiB a glyph engine, beside the 48 MiB cache that now holds documents only. `GlyphEngineProfile::plain_cache_bytes` and `plain_cache_budget` cover both, so the budget reads 4 MiB higher. The text caches of the chat screen hold 3.2 MB for its settled frame where they held 1.8 MB.
- `RenderProfile::draw_calls` is lower for a frame of several texts; the pixels are the same.
- `SelectTriggerState` has two new public fields, `leading` and `trailing`: code that builds one with every field named must add them.
- A backdrop blur is not run again while what is behind its element stays the same. The wgpu and the DX12 renderer keep the blurred pixels and put them back, with two draws inside the target's pass, in every frame in which the blur reads the same pixels: the same rectangle around the element's box at the same radius and scale, in the same window or layer, with the same things painted before the element where they can reach that rectangle. A frame that changes nothing behind any glass (a hover elsewhere, a caret, content over the glass, the glass's own opacity or corners) takes no copy, no blur pass and no pooled texture for it. A change behind a glass blurs that glass again, and whatever glass lies over it. Glass that can reach a video frame in a native texture, a `Canvas` or an external wgpu texture is blurred every frame, as before, since those change without the frame's draws changing. A window keeps at most 64 blurs, in textures of at most twice the bytes of its own pixels (1 MiB for a small window), and gives a texture back to the pool when its glass is gone, has moved or changed size, or the window is resized. The rule is spelled out in [Blur](docs/src/content/docs/blur.md#when-a-backdrop-is-blurred-again).
- A change of the hover asks for a frame only when it shows. The hover moves whenever the pointer comes onto another node (from a row onto the text inside it, for one), and every such move set `Tree::needs_redraw()`, so the pointer crossing a list of rows that look the same under it drew a frame for each move: 78 frames a second in `examples/hover_frames_probe.rs`, none of them different from the one before. A frame is now asked for when a node that gained or lost the hover has a `hovered` style (or any state style that changes frame, padding or flex, which lays it out again), is a checkbox, a slider, a select or a Markdown link, scrolls (its scrollbar may show for the pointer, and loses its hovered style with it), or ran an `on_mouse_enter` or `on_mouse_leave` handler; and for every change of the hover while DevTools is open on the window, which shows it. In the example the plain list draws 1 frame in 10 s (the pointer entering the list) and a list whose rows have a `hovered` style 38 a second, one per row the pointer comes onto, down from 78. The hover, its handlers, `Tree::cursor()`, `ElementRef::hovered()` and `InteractionState::is_hovered()` are up to date after every pointer move, as before. What relied on the frame a hover used to draw no longer gets one where the hover shows nothing: a shell that drives a `Tree` itself reads `Tree::cursor()` after each pointer event (the winit shell already does) rather than after a pass only, and a component that renders again on hover sets a signal from its hover handlers, since `ElementRef::hovered()` and `InteractionState::is_hovered()` are plain reads that render nothing.

Fixes:

- A variable font is drawn at the weight asked for. The platform's own font on a Mac and a phone is one variable face named Regular, and every weight was matched to that name, so nothing was bold.
- A scroll container's own padding was taken from its content's width and then not applied to where the content starts.
- A title bar given a height of its own is that high for the content under it.
- A wide blur against the window's left or right edge is no longer flat along it. A blur that runs on a reduced copy read the copy's outermost texel for the last half of it, so the pixels next to the window's edge repeated their neighbour: with a sharp edge 6 pixels from the window's, the first pixel under `backdrop_blur(24.0)` was 3 levels of 255 off. The reduced copy now has a texel of the repeated edge around it, and all four edges are read the same way.

Internal:

- `FrameSource` is the one place the shell asks for and takes frames, on every platform.
- The workspace builds and its tests pass on macOS and in an iOS simulator.
- The `lurq` package names what it publishes (`include` in its manifest), so a file put in the crate's directory stays out of a release unless it is in `src`, `assets`, `tests`, `benches` or `examples`.

The vertical Gaussian runs on the (reduced) copy into a texture of its own, as the horizontal one does, and the pass that composites a blurred layer or puts a backdrop back reads that texture once per pixel. Before, that pass ran the vertical Gaussian itself at the window's resolution, 25 reads per pixel, or 50 over a reduced copy (two Gaussians, interpolated): `backdrop_blur(32.0)` took about 56 texture reads per pixel of glass and takes about 14. A blur takes one more pass and one more pooled texture for it.

Every such write is a staging buffer and a copy at the frame's submit, and a faded group, a blurred layer or a glass that holds still has the same passes frame after frame: a frame that blurs four backdrops again took 16 writes for them and takes none.

Behaviour changes:

- None intended. Worth reporting: a layout that does not update after a change (a hover, focus or active style that changes size or padding, an element handle mutation, an animation, scrolling, text input); runtime state lost across a component's render (scroll offsets, text input state including styled spans, focus, element ids); a stack overflow on a deep tree.

## 0.50.0 — 2026-10-22

Behaviour changes:

- A `VirtualizedList` bootstraps from where it opens. Before it has measured a row it cannot size its window (the viewport is not laid out yet), so its first pass renders a fixed number of bootstrap rows whose heights seed the estimate the next pass sizes the window with. It rendered its first 64 rows, from the top, whatever the list was asked to open on, so a chat opened at its end (`scroll_to_bottom_pending` on its `ScrollState`) built and laid out its 64 oldest turns before rendering its newest, and did so again whenever another conversation was swapped in under it (no key survives, so the heights were cleared). It now renders 16 rows (`.bootstrap_rows(n)` sets another number), taken from the end when a scroll to the bottom is queued, from the `reveal_key`'s row when there is one, or else from the top. A list opened at its end builds its last 16 rows and none from its top, the same work for 300 rows and for 3,000; its first rows are built when they are scrolled to. When those rows are shorter than the viewport, the queued scroll is kept until the window is sized from their measured heights, so the list still lands at its end. A list opened at its top renders its first rows, as before, 16 instead of 64: a list of short rows fills its viewport one frame later than it did, and passes a larger `bootstrap_rows` if that frame matters. A list opened at an offset (a queued `set_scroll_pending`, or the offset a scroll state shared with the list before still holds) renders its first rows too, since no height names the row at that offset yet, and is scrolled to the offset once they are measured, again while the content sized from the estimate grows past it as the rows at its end are measured; it used to be clamped to the bootstrap rows and, as that was their end, follow to the real end. A scroll of the person's in between stands, and items swapped in with all-new keys drop the offset the list was still restoring for the rows before (one the app queued for the new rows stays). When the items are swapped for others whose keys all differ, the measured heights go but their average stays as the estimate, so the new rows are windowed where the list opens, about a viewport's worth, instead of bootstrapped from the top again.
- A context provided in `render` renders again only the descendants that read it. A component renders again for a context when a value of a type it read (`use_context`, `consume_context`, in `create`, a render or a handler) is provided anew, or when its own render provides values, since only a render provides them again; every other component keeps its rendered tree and passes the contexts on, so a reader below one renders in place with the pass and sees the new value; a `for_each` item that read the value (or provides) is rendered by its owner, whose render holds its closure. A component providing values in its render used to render every descendant again, read or not. `Tree::get_element_by_id`, `get_element_by_id_mut`, `get_elements_by_class_name` and the MCP element tree flush such readers too, refreshing again while a refresh replaced something (up to the eight refreshes a pass makes), so a lookup between passes sees the settled subtrees. `provide` still compares nothing: each call is a new providing, and the readers of that type render again; `Ctx::provide_if_changed` keeps the earlier providing while the value is equal (`PartialEq`), so nothing renders again for it.

Fixes:

- A `Markdown` document with a link in it kept the window's layout dirty: every idle pass found it dirty and laid the window out again, for as long as the document was shown (a chat showing a reply with a link relaid its window on every frame, ~2–3 ms in release). A link's node is placed by its paragraph's layout and holds no layout of its own, so the marks that ask for its layout (its anchor's attachment, a hover or focus style) were never consumed; the paragraph's layout now consumes them. A document without a link settled as before, and one with a link settles with it; a hovered link whose style keeps its metrics is painted, not laid out again.

Added:

- `VirtualizedList::bootstrap_rows(n)`: how many rows the list renders before it has measured one (16 by default).
- `Ctx::provide_if_changed(value)`: `provide`, unless the value equals the one this component provided before (`PartialEq`), in which case the descendants that read it see the same providing and do not render again. In `render` the value is compared with the previous render's; in `create` or a handler, with the one provided there.

## 0.48.2 — 2026-10-20

Fixes:

- A single-line text with an ellipsis (`TextOverflow::Elipsis`, `nowrap`) and a shrink factor in a Row or Column beside a growing child (a `Spacer::flex(1.0)`) was cut although its words fit, with the free room still beside it: "Pausing run #4 of KON-10 · dismiss…" in a box 1/16000 px narrower than its words. ## 0.24.2 — 2026-09-25

- Fix a UI-thread hang when a callback writes the signal it observes. `Signal::set` and `Signal::update` called the signal's subscribers while holding its value and subscriber locks. So a `ctx.watch` callback that set its own signal never returned, and neither did subscribing to or dropping a subscription of that signal from a callback. A common case was restarting a `FutureAction` from a watch on its own state: the completion set the state, the watch called `run`, and `run` set the state to `Pending`. Cooperative (non-Tokio) futures hung a second time, because `AsyncTask` was polled while holding the task lock that `run` takes. A notification now holds no lock while it calls observers, and the task lock is released before polling.

Every observer first receives the current value, then all of them are notified again with the latest value. Several writes during one notification produce one more notification. Writes from other threads during a notification are delivered by the thread that is already notifying. A watcher added during a notification starts with the next one. A watcher dropped during a notification is not called again.

  - A notification no longer copies the watcher list into a new `Vec`; observer lists are copy-on-write. A signal's value now sits behind an `Arc`, which costs one extra allocation per signal. `set` allocates only when a notification still borrows the old value.

The pressed element stayed active, and a text-selection, slider, scrollbar or `on_drag_*` session begun by the press kept following the pointer. The next release then completed the stale press and could fire a click or a drop. When `start_drag` or `start_resize` hands the press to a native loop, the winit shell now ends it with the new `Tree::mouse_press_taken_by_os`. ## 0.22.0 — 2026-09-23

Apple does not publish these colors; they are sampled from the system controls.

The new `ResizeHandlePlacement` (`WindowChromeProps::resize_placement`, `WindowChrome::resize_placement`) defaults to `Overlay`: the invisible edge and corner hit zones cover the outermost pixels of the content, which now fills the window below the title bar. Before, content and overlays were inset by the handle size on the left, right, and bottom, so a 1440-wide window gave 1434 px of content. `ResizeHandlePlacement::Inset` restores that layout. `WindowChromeMetrics::resize_inset` returns 0 for `Overlay`; the new `resize_handle_size_for(WindowInfo)` returns the active hit-zone size.

Add `WindowBorderColor` (`Default`, `None`, `Color`) with `WindowHandle::set_border_color` and `border_color`, mapped to `DWMWA_BORDER_COLOR` by the winit shell on Windows 11 build 22000+ and ignored elsewhere. `WindowChrome` sets `WindowBorderColor::None` when custom chrome is active; the frame outline comes only from `ChromeBorderPolicy`.
- Fix a faint 1px line on every window edge (and a soft 2px edge on every rect). The quad shaders (wgpu and DX12) average four half-pixel subsamples but gave each one a full-pixel anti-alias ramp, so a pixel entirely inside an axis-aligned edge got 87.5% coverage and the pixel outside got 12.5%. A window-sized root rect therefore let the clear colour show through its outermost pixels, maximized or not. Each subsample now uses a half-pixel ramp: pixel-aligned edges are exact, and curved and fractional edges stay anti-aliased. Rect edges throughout the UI render slightly crisper.
- Resolve the frame clear colour through the theme and past the overlay host. It was the root node's concrete background only, so a palette-coloured root, or any root while a `Modal`/overlay was mounted (which includes every `WindowChrome`), cleared to white.
- Fix live window resizing on Windows from custom chrome. Dragging a resize handle stretched the last frame for the whole drag, then froze the app for up to several seconds before it drew the new size. The shell started the native sizing loop with a synchronous `SendMessageW`, so the loop ran inside the lurq event handler, where winit queues every event. No frame was drawn during the drag, and afterwards the wgpu engine reconfigured its surface once per queued size event, which took about 2.4 ms each in a debug build. Now the shell posts the non-client press, at the real cursor position instead of (0, 0), so the loop runs from winit's pump. A `Resized` for the window's current size is painted immediately; WM_PAINT is starved during the loop. `WgpuRenderEngine::resize` records the size, and the next frame reconfigures the surface once. `start_drag` uses the same path. Present since custom chrome was added, not a regression.

## 0.20.0 — 2026-09-17

- Add gradient paints to `Context2D` fills and strokes: linear, radial and angular, 2 to 16 stops, with a centre, a size and a rotation normalised to a box. A gradient is a fragment-shader paint — one 256-texel ramp per distinct stop list, and paint coordinates carried in the vertex `uv` solid paths leave unused — so the mesh-cache key and the per-batch vertex charge are unchanged. An unusable gradient draws nothing rather than a colour of its own choosing, and is refused for shaped text with `UnsupportedPaint`.
- Add outer and inner shadows (`set_shadow`) and a `filter`-like layer blur (`set_filter`) to fills, strokes and shaped text. Both are rasterised once on the CPU into a premultiplied image and drawn like any other image, so the software backend and both native backends share one implementation. Bounded by a reduced raster for wide blurs, clipping to the surface, `MAX_EFFECT_PIXELS`, and 512-unit caps on blur and spread; rasterisations are cached process-wide at 32 MiB with random eviction. Offsets, blur and spread are in user space and scale with the transform, unlike HTML Canvas.
- Add eighteen blend modes through `set_global_composite_operation`, and isolated group compositing through `begin_layer(alpha, blend)` / `end_layer`, which is what makes a group of overlapping shapes fade as one image. Layers nest to `MAX_LAYER_DEPTH`, hold their commands on the surface until they close, and cost the renderer two shared tile-sized textures per open depth rather than a surface-sized target.
- Breaking: `fill_style()` and `stroke_style()` return `Paint` instead of `Color`; `Paint::color()` recovers the colour of a solid paint. The setters still accept `Color` and colour strings unchanged.
- Backdrop blur is not included: it reads the surface it writes, and its ordering guarantee is not yet stated. Tracked as lurq#17.
